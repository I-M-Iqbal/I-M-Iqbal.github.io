In October 2026, an inquest jury returned their verdict in the coroners inquest into the death of Akhtar Uddin, a 35 year old builder whom died while serving a sentence in HMP Wandsworth on Christmas Day 2021. The jury made the following conclusions:[^1]

- Uddin died due to actions he took but it was "not possible to determine whether he intended the outcome to be his death."
- "the imposition of the original TPIM and its unusually restrictive nature had a substantial impact on the mental state of Akhtar Uddin."
- "the fear of a likely reimposition of such a restrictive TPIM, and its possibly never-ending nature, had a significant impact on the mental state of Akhtar in prison."
- "We are satisfied, as a result of the consistent evidence that prison services were unaware of TPIM restrictions and their likely impact on Akhtar, there was a failure to provide support most suitably targeted to Akhtar's specific needs and situations." 

There was a  separate investigation by the prison ombusman into the prisons actions which concluded there were no signs that a risk of self harm was present which would have allowed prison staff to anticipate Mr Uddin's was at risk, prior to his death. The ombusman also found his treatment was comparable to the treatment he would have recieved for his mental health in the community.[^2] This report however, did not consider the impact of the TPIM on him nor prison staff lacking familiarity with TPIMs, both of which were considered by the inquest jury.[^2] 

The Coroner Mr Bernard Richmond KC said Uddin was "loved and loving" but was "not someone without his troubles" which included drug use.[^1] Richmond stated he would be issuing a report to be sent to the government about how to prevent further deaths from occurring as the result of the risk to TPIM subjects.[^1] 

The inquest verdict makes clear, Mr Uddin would not have died but for the decision to reimpose the TPIM when he was released from prison. This prison sentence arose from violating the terms of a prior TPIM that had been issued in January 2020. It is clear TPIMs are often cruel and appeals against them are arbitrary to such a degree, that the process becomes part of the punishment. 

Such measures today, following an amendment to the TPIM Act, can be imposed if the evidence supporting their imposition, meets the same legal standard required for arrest.[^3] Even Uddin's TPIM, imposed under a prior version of the TPIM Act, was imposed at a standard of proof closer to the civil standard than the criminal one.[^4] This is despite TPIMs being imposed for conduct that is likely to be covered by the many terror offences that already exists. 

Which begs a simple question: Why?

Following the 2010 election, the coallition government withdrew the law that permitted control orders and replaced them with Terrorism Prevention and Investigation Measures (TPIMs). TPIMs were meant to be a more proportionate, human rights compliant way of managing the risk posed by persons associated with terrorism who could not be prosecuted for criminal offences. However, many of the elements that made TPIMs better than control order have been ammended over time, with TPIMs today being near identical to Control Orders.

A Prevention of Future Death Report is sent by a Coroner, following an inquest, to those who could take steps to prevent furture deaths in the same or similar circumstances to the death at issue. For such a report to be sent to the government, means the inquest judge has determined they could take steps to prevent a death arising from the issuing of a TPIM.
